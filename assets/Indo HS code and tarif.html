Reading the numbers. Export Duty for palm products is a fixed USD/MT amount that steps through 17 reference-price bands; the levy is a flat percentage of the same CPO reference price, so it rises continuously. Both use the reference price published monthly by the Ministry of Trade — set it above to see the charges that actually apply to a shipment. Non-palm duty items are ad valorem percentages of export value and are shown as percentages rather than USD/MT. Cocoa steps with the cocoa reference price, not the CPO one.
  • Eight products are levied but carry no duty — EFB Oil, Used Cooking Oil, Soap Stock, Glycerine Water, the refined technical oils group, Split RBD Palm Oil-based, Split RBD Palm Kernel Oil-based and Crude Glycerine. This gap is exactly what the July 2026 BPDPKS harmonisation meeting identified as a circumvention risk, and it is the gap most likely to close in a future PMK.
  • The classification is still a draft. The Permenperin copy carries visible tracked changes and blank signature dates. Kemenperin confirmed in July 2026 that the revision to Permenperin 32/2024 is under way but not a priority, so expect movement — including a proposal to drop HS codes from the Permenperin altogether.
  • Source numbering defects. The classification annex numbers two separate products “10” in Group II, two “4” in Group IV and two “27” in Group V, and skips number 3 in Group III. Products are listed here in document order with those numbers as printed.
  • A shared HS code is not a shared charge. Both schedules key on product description and tariff post. Where an uncharged product shares a tariff post with a charged one, the row carries a warning — those are the positions where a declaration is most likely to be challenged and re-assessed.
